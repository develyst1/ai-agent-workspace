## What `end-of-day` actually does — the operational shape

- 🔴 **`end-of-day` is not a report; it WRITES.** It flips today's `CONFIRMED` bookings whose end time has passed to **`NO_SHOW`** and increments `used_sessions` / `used_hours` (Porter, from `jobs.service.ts:47–61`, 2026-08-23). ⚠️ See `## ⚠️ CONTRADICTIONS` — later entries describe the same job as *auto-attending*.
- 🔴 **Revenue posts ONLY for `ATTENDED`** (`jobs.service.ts:88`) while the cut turns unmarked `CONFIRMED` into `NO_SHOW` ⇒ **a session staff forget to mark is both consumed AND never posts revenue — silently** (Porter, 2026-08-23).
- **The cut selects `CONFIRMED` only.** `PENDING` is never cut — those rows sit forever (Porter, 2026-08-23). The job tidies one of the three states, not all.
- 🔴 **Re-running `end-of-day` for a PAST date is not a clean back-post:** for `runDate < today` the cut predicate is `sql\`true\`` (`jobs.service.ts:29`) ⇒ **every `CONFIRMED` booking on that date becomes `NO_SHOW` and consumes a session.** Nobody re-runs a past date on `uat` without a revenue-only mode (Porter, 2026-08-23; Sober queued it as a BE item the same day).
- **The job is idempotent on `rev:<bookingId>` — it cannot double-post** (Porter, 2026-08-23).
- 🔴 **`revenuePosted` in the `job_runs` row OVER-COUNTS**: `recordSale` returns `{ok:true, skipped:"duplicate"}` on a replay and the job counts `res.ok` ⇒ a re-run counts every already-posted sale again (Porter, from `lib/sale-post.ts:88`, 2026-08-23). **"What did we post today" read off `job_runs` is too high.** The ledger is the fact; the job's summary is a claim.
- 🔴 **`INTERNAL_JOB_SECRET` unset ⇒ the internal jobs endpoint returns `503 NOT_CONFIGURED` (`internal.ts:21`) and Windows Task Scheduler still reports SUCCESS** (Porter, 2026-08-23). ⇒ **the most likely silent failure on a fresh box; verify the manual run's response BODY, never the task's exit code.**
